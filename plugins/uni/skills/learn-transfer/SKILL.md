# Choose and apply in a new setting

Read [the tutoring contract](../learn/references/tutoring-contract.md). Identify the learned idea, prior performance and help, and intended application. Missing initial knowledge calls for instruction before an unsupported transfer test.

1. Privately check the problem, assumptions, solution, and alternatives. Vary structure, representation, context, constraints, or selection demand. Changing only numbers is usually practice on the same form.
2. Present one problem without naming the intended concept, source title, tags, or method cues. Ask for a choice and justification as one task. **End the turn and WAIT.**
3. Accept any valid method. Diagnose selection separately from execution: a good choice can have an arithmetic error, while recalled definitions may not support selection. Ask one missing decision at a time; teach explicitly when needed.
4. After the attempt, explain applicability, alternative methods, or the boundary of an analogy. Ask one fresh task that tests the repaired decision, then wait. Hinted selection is assisted transfer.
5. Record context, cueing, choice, execution, and help separately from recall. Limit conclusions to demonstrated tasks. One successful variant does not establish broad transfer; recall success does not erase transfer failure.

Mix related types when choosing among them serves discrimination. Do not shuffle everything or remove initial procedural support merely to increase difficulty. Small implementations, experiment designs, and nontechnical decisions can serve transfer with clear criteria.

“Harder” changes a meaningful demand; “smaller step” restores support; “just explain” gives instruction. Stop or pause immediately when requested. Use [learn-review](../learn-review/SKILL.md) for further concept drills within the chat.

See [the evidence matrix](../learn/references/evidence-matrix.md) for material-dependent interleaving and contingent retrieval-to-transfer evidence. This is a teaching design, not a promise of general expertise.
