# Retrieve and diagnose

Read [the tutoring contract](../learn/references/tutoring-contract.md), especially privacy and evidence rules. Inputs are a concept or exercise, scope, sources, and budget. Keep explicit requests for question banks or answer sheets distinct from interactive administration.

## Prepare and ask

Privately prepare a checked reference answer, essential criteria, acceptable equivalents, partial answers, and likely misconceptions before asking. Use internal reasoning. Verify factual material or math/code reasoning as needed before selecting a prompt; do not expose a solution-bearing tool result before the attempt. Source titles, tags, and wording must not reveal the method or answer. If a reliable key cannot be prepared without exposure, defer the item and state the verification limitation without revealing its solution.

Choose one purposeful task: free recall, explanation, prediction, derivation, debugging, counterexample, discrimination, or application. Multiple choice can serve a diagnostic purpose; do not use it as the default substitute for generation. Request confidence occasionally before feedback when useful.

**Ask one question, end the turn, and WAIT for the real learner response.** Never simulate their answer, grade an absent answer, or continue the dialogue. Preserve the first response before hints.

## Grade and adapt

1. Judge substance against the prepared criteria. Accept equivalent notation and valid alternative methods. Separate omitted detail from a wrong mechanism. Confidence and chat latency do not determine correctness.
2. Explain an error and why it matters. Give a suitable hint or explicit correction, then choose one follow-up based on the actual error. A copied correction is not delayed retrieval.
3. If the key is wrong, ambiguous, or credibly disputed, suspend grading and verify. Correct your assessment; do not penalize the learner. Repair or replace a defective question.
4. Keep the first response, initial correctness, and help in view when judging progress. Use [learn-review](../learn-review/SKILL.md) for further concept drills within the chat.

“Hint” uses the ladder. “Just explain” or “show the answer” gives the answer now and marks exposure. “Skip” leaves the question ungraded. “Stop” stops questions. A chat handoff summary must omit the pending answer.

Read [simulated examples](../learn/references/examples.md) for the turn boundary and [the evidence matrix](../learn/references/evidence-matrix.md) for retrieval and feedback qualifications.
