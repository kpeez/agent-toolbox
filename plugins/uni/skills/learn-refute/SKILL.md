# Criticize and repair

Read [the tutoring contract](../learn/references/tutoring-contract.md). Start from the learner's claim or argument, domain, and assumptions. If no claim exists, ask for one bounded conjecture or explanation and **WAIT**.

1. Restate the claim faithfully, including quantifiers. Privately check truth and argument separately. Clarify only interpretations that change the test.
2. Invite one example, edge case, or counterexample that tests a consequential premise. Leave real reasoning for the learner. When stuck, increase help or demonstrate the test.
3. Locate the failing statement or inference. Distinguish “the conclusion is false” from “this argument does not establish the conclusion.” A true theorem can have a flawed proof; never manufacture a refutation of a valid theorem.
4. Help revise the statement, definition, assumptions, or reasoning. Accept valid alternative repairs. Name the cost of restricting a domain. Ask for one retest under the repaired formulation and wait.
5. Consolidate what changed and what remains unproved. Preserve the first attempt and help before recording the correction.

For empirical claims, identify the model, observation process, uncertainty, and predicted likelihood. A surprising sample is not itself a logical counterexample to a probabilistic model. Consider measurement error, sampling variation, and mismatch. For code, distinguish a failing implementation from a false algorithmic claim; bounded tests can help.

If your key or counterexample is uncertain, suspend grading and verify. Check your interpretation and key when the learner challenges them. Ordinary controls take effect immediately under the contract.

This is a Lakatos-inspired teaching adaptation, not a validated universal protocol. [Research notes](../learn/references/research-notes.md) distinguish historical argument from pedagogical adaptation. A useful chat handoff summary names the repaired claim, uncertainty, and next test.
