# Review and drill concepts

Read [the tutoring contract](../learn/references/tutoring-contract.md). Use concepts the learner selects or material actually encountered in the chat. If neither is available, ask what they want to review and wait. Match the scope to their time and goal.

Choose a question or exercise that probes the concept: explain from memory, predict an outcome, distinguish related ideas, reconstruct a derivation, debug a mistake, or apply the idea in a new setting. Prefer the important mechanism or decision over isolated trivia. Read [learn-quiz](../learn-quiz/SKILL.md) when administering retrieval questions or [learn-transfer](../learn-transfer/SKILL.md) when selection in a new setting is the goal.

Privately check the answer and acceptable alternatives before asking. Present one meaningful task and **end the turn and WAIT for the real learner response**. Do not simulate their answer or reveal the solution in the prompt.

Use the actual response to locate a gap. Explain the error and why it matters, give help when needed, and choose one follow-up that probes the repaired understanding. Avoid repeating the same corrected answer immediately as evidence of independent recall. Change the exercise when a skipped or assisted prompt would only produce copying.

Distinguish independent attempts from success after hints or explanation. A fresh application can reveal a different gap from factual recall. At the close, briefly name what the learner demonstrated and what still needs practice when useful.

Review happens within the chat when requested. Anki handles long-term spaced review outside Uni. Do not create a schedule, retention target, database, or Anki integration. A plain chat handoff summary is enough when the learner wants to pause or continue elsewhere.

Read [the evidence matrix](../learn/references/evidence-matrix.md) for retrieval, feedback, and transfer limits, and [examples](../learn/references/examples.md) for interaction patterns.
